*Chronostasis* is named for the perceptual illusion where the first
moment after your eyes snap to something new feels stretched out, as if a
clock's second hand had briefly frozen. This film was my final project for
Still and Moving Images at the end of my first year of college, and it
carries that idea into the frame: a study of time that seems to hesitate
before it moves again.

At its core, the film is about being controlled by time versus learning to
control it. It follows a character moving through their day at the mercy of
the clock, rushed from one moment to the next, until they realize they can
slow down and appreciate the small things — in this case, nature — rather
than let time dictate the pace.

It was my first real attempt at treating a short film as a formal
exercise — building a visual language out of stillness and motion rather
than plot alone, and seeing how far a small idea could stretch when every
shot has to earn its place.
