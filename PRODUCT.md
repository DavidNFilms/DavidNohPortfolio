# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary visitors are creative-industry peers, collaborators, mentors, and curious
people in tech, media, education, and the arts — plus the occasional recruiter or
studio. They arrive having seen David's work somewhere (a reel, a social post, a
referral) and want to gauge the range and quality of it in a couple of minutes,
then find a way to reach out. They're browsing on everything from a phone between
classes to a large desktop display. The job to be done: form a fast, vivid
impression of David as a multidisciplinary visual storyteller, see the work itself,
and feel invited to connect.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for **David Noh** — filmmaker, photographer, and XR designer,
currently studying Integrated Design & Media at NYU Tandon. It exists to showcase
the work (Films, Photo, XR) as the hero and to open doors for conversation and
collaboration. Success is a visitor who leaves with a clear sense of David's craft
and point of view, remembers the work, and reaches out. This is a showcase-and-network
surface, not a transactional or lead-funnel site; the goal is impression and
connection, not conversion metrics.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, confident, and crafted. The voice is warm and personal in copy ("Hi! I'm
David… Let's connect!") while the visual treatment is restrained and filmic — like
the title card and color grade of a well-shot short. Three words: **cinematic,
considered, inviting.** The interface should evoke the quiet authority of a darkened
screening room: the work is lit, everything else recedes. Personality comes through
in the imagery, the oxblood accent, and the typographic confidence — never through
decoration for its own sake.

## Anti-references

- **Generic template portfolios.** Squarespace/Wix stock layouts, identical icon-
  heading-text card grids, anything that reads as "picked a theme." The work and the
  craft of presentation are the differentiator; never let it look interchangeable.
- **Bright / playful / startup energy.** Light backgrounds, pastel gradients, bubbly
  SaaS marketing tropes. This is a dark, cinematic surface; warmth is carried by the
  accent and imagery, not by a cheerful palette.
- **Corporate / sterile agency look.** Cold, faceless, point-of-view-free. There is a
  real person here with a real voice; the design should feel authored, not committee-made.

## Design Principles

- **The work is the light; the UI is the dark.** Imagery and reels carry the page.
  Chrome, type, and color stay quiet so the photography and film never compete with
  the interface around them.
- **Cinematic over decorative.** Every effect should read like a deliberate filmic
  choice (grade, vignette, title card, slow drift), not an added flourish. If a touch
  doesn't serve the screening-room mood, cut it.
- **Authored, not templated.** Sharp corners, Swiss hairlines, sequence numerals, and
  a self-hosted display face are identity. Hold the system; resist generic portfolio
  patterns even when they're easier.
- **Show range, then invite.** Lead with breadth of craft (Films / Photo / XR), close
  with a warm, low-friction way to connect. Impression first, conversation second.
- **One voice across many surfaces.** Home, About, and each project section can be art-
  directed differently, but the cinematic-considered-inviting voice stays constant.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Looks-first by the owner's direction: the cinematic impression takes priority, and
artistic low-contrast moments over imagery (hero overlays, credit badges, muted
metadata) are acceptable where they serve the mood. That said, keep the easy wins
that don't fight the art — the existing `prefers-reduced-motion` handling stays,
nav and links remain keyboard-operable, and alt text on real imagery is part of the
voice. Treat WCAG AA on core body copy and navigation as a target to hold where it
costs nothing visually, not a hard constraint on the art direction.
